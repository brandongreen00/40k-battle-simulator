[40K] 11th Edition Points Review: Imperium Factions

by James "One_Wing" Grover, TwoHorse, Carter "Saffgor" Kachmarik, Garrett Moore, MildNorman | Jun 17 2026
The final piece of the 11th Edition puzzle is revealed today, with the Munitorum Field Manual landing and providing points costs and Force Dispositions for all factions. Our team have dug in to analyse what this means for the early 11th Edition metagame in our massive review. This part focuses on the Space Marine factions.

Thanks to Games Workshop for providing us with early access to these rules. Any details we mention are subject to change until published on Warhammer Community or the Warhammer 40K App.

Welcome to our review of Imperium Faction points for 11th Edition. As it's a whole new world out there, most factions in the game get lots of changes, so we're not going to try and list every single one. For each faction, we're focusing on the changes that we think will have the biggest impact on listbuilding, and covering off where we think the army lands in the new edition.

You can find the rest of our review, covering other factions and a general overview, via the links below:

  • Overview

  • Chaos Factions
  • Xenos Factions

Adepta Sororitas

Sisters had a very strong Codex in 10th with some exceptional builds out the gate, got over-nerfed as punishment, but were eventually reset to a pretty happy medium by later changes, with strong but not dominant performance in the closing months of 10th. Let's see how that’s reflected in the new edition.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Battle Sisters Squad: -5pts
  • Dominion Squad: -20pts. 10pt tax on the 3rd unit. Each Meltagun costs 5 points. The most common Dominion configuration of 4x meltagun has stayed the same price, but there’s a bit more incentive to run the other weapon options now.
  • Exorcist: -30pts for the first unit, down to 180. Tax of 40pts on the second. This is interesting because Exorcists were previously playable at 180, but at 220 pts for the second/third Exorcists multiples are less attractive. This does mean that taking a single for use in the Chorus of Condemnation isn’t as absurd as it first looked - you still won’t, but you’re not an idiot for considering it.
  • Mortifiers: -10pts for a squad of 2.
  • Paragon Warsuits: -30pts. 10pt tax on the 3rd unit. Each multi-melta costs 10pts. The same price if you run them in their most common configuration, but the enlightened among us will enjoy the buff to Glorious Heavy Bolter Vahlgons.
  • Penitent Engines: -5/-10pts.

Nerfs

  • Aestred Thurga and Agathae Dolan: +10pts.
  • Arco Flagellants: +5pts for 3; unchanged at 150pts for 10
  • Castigator: +5pts. Tax of 10pts on the 3rd unit.
  • Celestian Sacresants: +5/10pts for 5/10 models. Tax of 15pts on the second unit. This is a significant hit. The new Sacresants-focused detachment and the ability to double-attach characters combine to make Sacresants an attractive take, but leaning heavily into them is suddenly very expensive.
  • Hospitaller: +15pts. Tax of 10pts on the 2nd. The ability to revive bodyguard models after they’re all dead is clearly top of mind for GW; Hospitaller utility is much improved in Hallowed Martyrs, but without the ability to revive her this price is extremely steep.
  • Immolator: -5 pts, +15pts for the twin multi-melta, tax of 10pts for your fourth unit. This is a net increase of 10pts for the most common configuration despite appearing as a “buff” due to the base discount. The 5pt discount along with the impact of cover in 11e means that flamers may see some use now, but we believe this is effectively a nerf.
  • Junith Eruita: +35 pts. This one is a bit gratuitous. Sure, she provides -1 to hit which stacks with cover, but the units she joins are still T3 1W bodies, and they’ve also gotten points increases.
  • Retributor Squad: -15pts, +5pts per Multi-melta, +10pt tax on your third unit. Another “buff” in which the most common configuration goes up in price.
  • Seraphim Squad: +5/0 pts, up to 85pts for 5 and still 160 for 10. Tax of 10pts on your third unit. Another headscratcher, given that the new 11e prohibition on shoot+scoot after arriving via Deep Strike has killed their ability to threaten primary scoring from reserves. If anything their price should be going down, so this one makes no sense to us.
  • Triumph of Saint Katherine: +10pts.

Force Dispositions

3 DP Detachments
  • Bringers of Flame - Purge the Foe
  • Hallowed Martyrs - Priority Assets
2 DP Detachments
  • Army of Faith - Take and Hold
  • Champions of Faith - Disruption (Unique: Reverend)
  • Penitent Host - Take and Hold (Why?)
1 DP Detachments
  • Chorus of Condemnation - Reconnaissance
  • Sacred Champions - Take and Hold (Unique: Reverend)
  • Sanctified Orators - Purge the Foe

Outlook

Neutral

Thoughts

TwoHorse: I’ll start by saying that this is fine probably; Sisters will remain playable. Top Sisters players will probably take down an event or two, but these costs ensure they’re going to have to work for it. Without putting too fine a point on it, this feels like a fairly specific build overperformed in some phase of testing, and got significantly toned down as a result, then didn’t get as much of a second pass as some other armies.

It isn’t that the nerfed units haven’t gotten better in 11th edition - most of them have - it’s that the same philosophy does not appear to have been applied elsewhere. We won’t waste your time enumerating the absurd comparisons to be made if you look at the Necrons or Space Marine MFMs, but suffice it to say the Sisters points changes reflects an imminently reasonable philosophy of how 11th edition might work, and we hope that the next points adjustment brings more factions in line with that version of the game.

On a positive note, there are some interesting new options. Exorcists (only one) and pairs of Mortifiers may make an appearance now that their pricing is more reasonable, and the changes to Battle-shock, indirect, cover, and fight-on-death make both slightly better than they were in 10th edition. The Hospitaller may be ludicrously priced, but bolting her onto a unit of Sacresants with Junith or Battle Sisters with the Triumph creates a genuinely durable unit of the sort Sisters could not previously access. Are those units worth 340 or 420pts respectively? Certainly we don’t think so, but we’ve been wrong before.

Adeptus Custodes

Custodes had a dire 10th Edition Codex launch, but various improvements over time furnished them with a range of valid builds, and they were particularly successful with Land Raider and Custodian-heavy Talons builds late in the day.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Vertus Praetors: -5/10pts (2/3 models). Bikes get cheaper alongside the changes to FLY and the introduction of boards that should be pretty favourable to them. The Dawneagle Captain also goes down, and they bring an important ability to the table by allowing a unit to move onto terrain, shoot through it, charge something on it, then move back off once complete. There has been a gradual rise in the number of lists packing one unit of these anyway, expect that to increase further. The AP-1 D2 bolters are also pretty lit with the new Cover rules.
  • Dawneagle Shield-Captain: -10pts. See above.
  • Trajann Valoris: -5pts. It’s a small change, but the way steppers are applied to Blade Champions (see below) means that more lists are probably going to end up taking Trajann, so being slightly cheaper is no bad thing.
  • Venatari: -5/10pts. These were good anyway, there’s more pressure not to spam stuff via steppers, so you probably take them more, especially if they’re cheaper.
  • Your first Blade Champion: -10pts. One in every list then. Units 2+ go to +5pts instead, which is still takeable.

Also very relevant is that Allarus are unchanged, which given they’re very good, and supported by a decent 1DP Detachment, so expect to see plenty of them.

Nerfs

  • Custodian Guard: +10/15pts (units 1-3), +20/25pts (units 4+). In the cold light of the dawn of a new edition, someone looked at how stupid cheap these have gotten and went “hang on”, so they take a general hit, and the lists on 4+ units take a much bigger one. This will definitely force a re-tooling of some builds, as 4-6 units has been pretty common. They’re still strong, especially as the AP-1 on their double shooting will be a lot more impactful, though the flip side is that they die a lot faster to many similar weapons.
  • Blaze Cannon Caladius: +10pts (+20pts for your third). The Custodes main answer to enemy armour goes up slightly in price, and is generally a bit weaker with the new Cover rules. Also gets a stepper at three, but two has always been the sweet spot so that doesn't really matter.
  • Land Raiders: +20pts (units 2+). The Custodian/Land Raider spam lists get tapped a second time here, adding a decent penalty if you take more than one. An un-nerfed version of those builds could have been pretty nightmarish in 11th so this is probably healthy for the game, but a definite hit.
  • Custodian Wardens: +20pts (units 2+). People had largely stopped taking more than one unit of Wardens anyway, and yeah don’t do that.
  • 4-model Sisters units: +5pts. The price of getting cheap utility down goes up a little. You want some in every list, so it’s a legitimate tax.

Force Dispositions

3 DP Detachments
  • Talons of the Emperor - Take and Hold
2 DP Detachments
  • Auric Champions - Priority Assets
  • Lions of the Emperor - Disruption (Unique: Lions)
  • Null Maiden Vigil - Recon
  • Shield Host - Purge the Foe
  • Solar Spearhead - Take and Hold (Unique: Armoury)
1 DP Detachments
  • Might of the Moritoi - Purge the Foe (Unique: Armoury)
  • Silent Hunters - Recon
  • Tharanatoi Hammerblow - Priority Assets (Unique: Lions)

Outlook

Neutral

Thoughts

Wings: Custodes should do fine in the new edition, and I’d lean towards the upper end of average. There was probably a world where none of these nerfs happened and they ended up definitively strong, but slamming down the current best build is costing you 100pts+ extra, and that plus their reduced durability in the face of AP-1 firepower is probably enough to keep them balanced (though if they swing hard either up or down, I’d expect it to be up).

In terms of what armies look like, the points changes do feel like they railroad the army a bit towards a pretty consistent toolbox build. You probably take one Warden unit, one Blade Champion, one Bike/Captain squad and so on, because many units are great value on the first buy, less so on the second. I think you probably can still run a two Land Raider version of Custodians/Raiders, and the question is whether you keep it as Talons, or add Terminators and go Shield Host/Tharanatoi so you can have Purge (or even add some Dreads and go Moritoi). Either way, I’m pleased that there are some trade-offs around the obviously over-dominant build now, and we’ll see where Custodes end up.

Adeptus Mechanicus

The Adeptus Mechanicus also had a rough Codex, and languished in the doldrums for a long time, but eventually a major re-write of many units and rules, plus the addition of the powerful all-rounder Haloscreed Battle-Clade helped them do very well in the final year of 10th.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Ironstrider Ballistarii (with Twin cognis autocannon): -5/10/15pts for units 1-2. Hope you magnetized your walkers. Ironstriders were a mainstay of the faction in 10th, and while their chosen loadout might be changing, you’ll still be seeing plenty of chickens. That said, efficient S12 shooting is required in the army, so it’s important to fill the niche lascannon Ironstriders are leaving.
  • Thulia Ghuld: -30pts. Thulia is an autoinclude in any list taking Lords of the Forge, especially at these points. Outside of Lords, she’s still a fine pick, but with a 5+++ Thulia is more than able to stick around far longer. One of the big reasons you’d take Lords.
  • Kastelan Robots: -20/40pts for unit 1 (and -10pts for the Datasmith). Lords+Cohort Cybernetica looks to be one of the strongest initial builds for a 1+2 DP list, and many players might look to include a bot squad & Vingh’s Wafers of Dynamism for 25pts to enable that essential brick. There’s never a world where taking 2+ units is correct outside of casual play, but as a midboard bully few things are scarier than mobile Kastelans (if you can set aside 370pts for the package). I have a hunch these get crunched out once people truly optimize, but they’re closer to playability than anytime in 10th.
  • Hastarii Fusileers: -30pts for units 1-2. Look, Hastarii were a joke after they debuted in 10th, but this does much to rectify it. Sticking 2x5 Fusileers in a Dunerider (or Transvector, for the jank enjoyers) makes for an efficient source of close-range S12 shots. That’s a fragile package, especially for 315pts, but will absolutely murder whatever their target is.
  • Kataphron Destroyers: -5pts for 3-model units. This is a small buff, but one that comes alongside some massive changes surrounding hazardous. For a cool 100pts, you can walk-on 12 4+/8/-3/2 shots and some small-arms fire to taste; the cost is so low, and the unit is just tough enough not to die to things like bolters, that these make for solid Damage 2 trade pieces that don’t take up the area of an Onager Dunecrawler.

Nerfs

  • Servitor Battleclade: +5pts. One of the most underrated units in the entire game got hit with a slap on the wrist, but given you’ll be taking 2-3 in any Lords of the Forge list it’s a bit more substantial. This could chase it out of things like Haloscreed however, which lack the new upside of having a TECH-PRIEST model.
  • Ironstrider Ballistarii (with Twin cognis lascannon); Skorpius Disintegrator (with Ferrumite cannon): +10pts/model. I feel it important to batch these because the changes made do the same thing: Tax Admech’s access to S12 shooting. As our main victims of granular weapon points, punching through key threats is getting a bit more expensive in 11th, and there’s ample need to find offsets. Ultimately, while Laschickens probably lose out against their Autocannon version, Cybernetica lists are still likely to require Disintegrators.
  • Belisarius Cawl: +10pts. This is just a tax to play the army, and I doubt Cawl will leave any list in 11th. Annoying, but moving the needle on Cawl affects literally every Admech list, so it’s good to see it only be a 10pt hike.
  • Pteraxii Skystalkers: +5pts for 5-model units, +15/10pts for unit 3. Skystalkers make up one third of the soffrito of Admech’s secondary play pieces, being themselves, Infiltrators, & Raiders. They already ate a nerf with the lack of movement after shooting, when arriving via an Ingress move, so to see that coupled with point nerfs is a rough double whammy. You may consider 5, but as their cost approaches that of the Callidus Assassin, that ally becomes more appetizing.
  • Sicarian Infiltrators; Sicarian Ruststalkers: +10pts for unit 3, +5pts for unit 3. These actually saw some slight decreases in cost when taken in their first or second squad, but the designers have made it clear that the days of 45-60 Sicarian bodies are long gone. This is another nerf to detachments like Skitarii Hunter Cohort, and probably pushes Haloscreed to include just the 2x10 Ruststalkers, and never a third Infiltrator squad.

Force Dispositions

3 DP Detachments
  • Eradication Cohort - Purge the Foe
  • Haloscreed Battle Clade - Purge the Foe)
2 DP Detachments
  • Cohort Cybernetica - Take and Hold
  • Data-Psalm Conclave - Disruption (Unique: Data-Psalm)
  • Explorator Maniple - Priority Assets
  • Rad-zone Corps - Take and Hold
  • Skitarii Hunter Cohort - Recon
1 DP Detachments
  • Cohort Acquisitus - Recon
  • Lords of the Forge - Priority Assets
  • Luminen Auto-Choir - Disruption (Unique: Data-Psalm)

Outlook

Neutral

Thoughts

Saffgor: First thing to note, this is overall a very conservative slate of point changes, outside of exactly our most recent release, with Thulia & the Hastarii. Those are fairly major shifts, and I fully expect to see Thulia nearabouts everywhere and 2x5 Fusileers in a Dunerider with or without Marshals. There’s a scant few datasheets left in Admech that are truly unplayable (even the Skatros has potential as the bearer of Radial Suffusion), and that’s been a steep climb since the codex in 10th.

After some testing and talking, I feel fairly confident in saying the two top options for Admech are Haloscreed, and the nebulous ‘Best Lords + 2 DP option’ list. That second one is said in the confidence something will be found, given the strength of Lords, and it’s really just a matter of limit testing to see what its preferred partner is. Anecdotally, the best place to start is likely Cohort Cybernetica (without Kastelans) in a reactive, conservative Protector Doctrina list with 2x3 Autochickens and 2-3 Disintegrators. The change to shrugged damage is a huge buff to their best enhancement, Necromechanic, and you can both ignore the issues with cover and careen up the board with Vehicles by way of their stratagems. It isn’t flashy, but the sustain available at range means you can coast on the necessary ~1 action per turn via Battleclade & Raiders, and dunk opponents who peek out.

Still, I believe there’s reasons to consider Rad-Zone Corps & Explorator Maniple as viable options; both of these offer fairly generic benefits, and leave plenty of room for flexibility in your listbuilding once you pay the Lords tax of Thulia, Cawl, & 2-3 Battleclade. Neither detachment rule is flashy, each has an enhancement that creates a deadly unit emerging from a boat (Peerless Eradicator & Artisan, respectively) and has ~2 stratagems that come up more than once in a Martian moon. The thing is though, 7 games into 11th…I’ve personally felt best about Explorator (after Halo). It has gained substantial contextual strength, with objectives taking up a greater chunk of the table (and being inherently more important, as terrain) on top of squeezing plenty of juice from the Support change to things like the Technoarchaeologist & Skitarii Marshal. The stack of Vanguard, Inquisitor Greyfax, & an Artisan Techno gives you efficient point-and-click precision mortals, on top of stratagems to sticky on death & reactively reembark. In many ways, it’s essentially Eradication Cohort with access to Priority Targets & Lords’ buffs (meaning it’s better Eradication). For the early events I’ll be attending, I intend to play Halo in teams, & Explorator solo, so take that as you will. Many of the coolest things arriving with 11th serve to buff what was previously Admech’s least-played detachment, and I think folks should be giving it a fair shake if only to see how the context of the new edition is shaping objective-oriented strategies.

Agents of the Imperium

Agents of the Imperium made a return as a full faction in 10th…kind of. The book was awkward outside of one genuinely great detachment (which a few dedicated pilots took to some excellent finishes), and GW didn’t seem to know what to do with them after that. This does not, unfortunately, seem to have changed as of yet.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • None

Nerfs

  • Immolator: +15pts with multi-meltas (both mono-faction and as Agents). Ouch on that one.
  • Grey Knight Terminators: +5pts/psycannon. No one ever took these, keep not doing that.

Force Dispositions

3 DP Detachments
  • Imperialis Fleet - Disruption
  • Ordo Hereticus, Purgation Force - Purge the Foe
  • Ordo Malleus, Daemon Hunters - Priority Assets
  • Ordo Xenos, Alien Hunters - Purge the Foe
  • Veiled Blade Elimination Force - Recon

Outlook

Loser

Thoughts

Wings: Legitimately, what are we even doing here? Take a faction that’s already under-supported and under-equipped and nerf its only anti-tank gun? I am genuinely baffled by the lack of effort on Agents for 11th’s launch, because to my mind they would be fun to write some 1DP Detachments for. Make Veiled Blade 1DP so you can have super Assassins and a functioning Imperialis Fleet build. Let them take 500pts of Militarum Tempestus. Hell, let them take 500pts of Chaos Daemons, because there has been another certified Inquisitor Eisenhorn moment. They’re in such a bad spot that it’s almost impossible that you break the game by getting really out here with it, and surely there must be some fun, joy and whimsy you can have as a game designer at that point?

Anyway - you probably can have a semi-decent go at Disruption with Imperialis Fleet, and I will be sure to say very nice things about anyone who manages a 4-1 with the game’s weakest faction and weakest Force Disposition.

Astra Militarum

Guard have been perpetually strong in 10th. Their Codex provided all the tools to play Good Warhammer, and the Grizzled Company detachment from the final Grotmas batch was exceptionally good, keeping them in the conversation right to the end of the edition.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Commissar Yarrick: -20pts. Some recognition here that nerfing Yarrick into the ground before he got to do anything was maybe a bit harsh. Still not sure he’s worth it at the new price, but it’s closer.
  • Gaunt’s Ghosts: -5pts. On the big list of things that get a small cut that you were taking anyway. I guess the thinking is that Hidden makes their Lone Operative less unique? Keep taking them anyway.
  • Lord Marshall Dreir: -25pts. This gets Dreir to a point where he’s decent value as a semi-sticky utility/scoring piece, especially as the price on a bunch of other cheap stuff goes up. Death Riders’ reactive move is very powerful in many matchups too, and at 135pts for the combined unit, you could see giving him a squad to ride with.
  • Centaur RSV: -10pts. Gives you a more compelling reason to take it over a Chimera if you’re squeezed on points.
  • Your first Basilisk: -25pts. I think if you have a source of Take Aim for a single Basilisk, you seriously consider taking one at this price, even with the hits Indirect Fire has taken. You can get it hitting on 4s, and its AP-2 is a lot scarier than it used to be.

In the land of things that don’t change in a helpful way, your first squad of 6 Bullgryn doesn’t increase in price (and the stepper on a second isn’t too bad) and your first two Hellhounds stay the same, which is good because you’re gonna be needing those.

Nerfs

OK - there are a lot of these, often as part of stepped costs. You probably need to go read this yourself. If we have not included whichever one you, personally, are most mad about, that’s because we’re out to get you. 
  • All Platoon Infantry Squads: +10pts/25pts (except Krieg who don’t change on large units). Whether you want lots of small units for scoring or big units as centrepieces, you’re paying more.
  • Tempestus Scions: +5/15pts (first two units) +15/25pts (units 3+). Scions are a great all-purpose damage-dealing option, and this adds significant cost to going hard on them. Militarum Tempestus Command Squads also get +10pts for units after the first. The Taurox Prime does go down by 5pts, so expect to see more lists doing 5s with Commands in Tauroxes to shave some points.
  • Kasrkin/5-model Combat Engineers: +5pts (first two units) +15pts (third unit). Lists were absolutely running three of each of these. Now they probably won’t.
  • Leman Russ Vanquisher: +5pts (+15 for third). Vanquishers got too cheap, and were being spammed as threes a lot.
  • Rogal Dorns/Dorn Commander: +15pts (unit 2+). One of each it is, then.

Force Dispositions

3 DP Detachments
  • Combined Arms - Take and Hold
  • Grizzled Company - Priority Assets (Unique: Abhuman)
  • Recon Element - Recon (duh) (Unique: Recon)
2 DP Detachments
  • Armoured Infantry - Take and Hold
  • Hammer of the Emperor - Purge the Foe
  • Mechanised Assault - Purge the Foe
  • Siege Regiment - Disruption
  • Steel Hammer - Purge the Foe
1 DP Detachments
  • Abhuman Auxiliaries - Take and Hold (Unique: Abhuman)
  • Bridgehead Strike - Priority Assets
  • Designation Force - Recon (Unique: Recon)

Outlook

Neutral

Thoughts

Wings: This one I’m torn on, because prior to seeing points Guard were on the list of factions I was pretty worried about rolling people early on. It turns out so was the team that came up with these prices, because this is pretty brutal.

Any real Guard build is going to be down at least one, often two units, and that’s potentially enough of a reduction to their board presence that aspects of 11th that hurt them bite harder. While their tanks poking their noses into terrain and shooting through is nasty, those same tanks aren’t sporting invulnerable saves, so against any foes with AP-3+ weaponry and good re-rolls, they’re suddenly quite a lot more fragile. New Cover also hurts their output as a BS4+ army, because sure Orders partially counters it, but the drop off is still real, so I expect Hellhounds to reappear in lists at breakneck speed to help handle that.

Even with that in mind, I don’t think that adds up to Guard being bad, and I can believe they’re still quite good, but I do think it’s probably enough to keep them out of the upper echelons of the game, as I think Marines and T’au in particular are just going to be able to beat them at their own game.

Grey Knights

Grey Knights have exactly one great Detachment in Warpbane Task Force, and a few good Datasheets to use it with. Whether they have been good has essentially been a function on whether this plan is acceptable into the metagame. In late 10th, it has not been.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Base Unit Terminators and Paladins: -10pts on 4 and 5 man Paladins and Brotherhood Terminators. This is nice to see, especially because of how cover changes really hurt 2+ armor saves. It is nice to shave a few points where you can, and both of these units only see taxes after a few options, namely 4 or more for Terminators and 3 or more for Paladins. These “buffs,” however, are pretty conditional on the nerfs that I will dive into below.
  • Chassis of Dreadknights: -25pts on the Grand Master, -15pts on the Dreadknight. This is on the chassis only, so this is a “buff” in only the strictest sense, as the good options for ranged weapons will still require the same or more points. However, this means if you want to shove in some less good options and still bring the unit, you can save a few points.
  • Land Raider Redeemer: -20pts. This is solid, it brings the Redeemer back into the land of reasonable points cost for the first one. However, the second and onward is still sitting at 270, which means it’s probably not headed back to the days of six Dreadknights and three Redeemers.

Nerfs

  • Psycannons: +5pts per Psycannon for Brotherhood Terminators, Paladins, Purgation Squads, Purifier Squads. +15 for all variants of Heavy psycannon, plus an additional +15 for Sublimators. This essentially negates all of the point cuts for units described above, as these are by far the best option available, especially with Ignores Cover flamers being in a limbo of uselessness. This means that everywhere I described a buff above, it is as best a wash if you take the best loadouts. While I do like the idea of costs of wargear being separate, I feel like the only good wargear available shouldn’t be taxed quite this hard.
  • Brotherhood Librarian: +10pts. This is probably tied to the pseudo Lone Op this guy gives, but he really didn’t need a nerf. In the world of Gone to Ground and Hidden, is 18” Lone Op really that spicy? Additionally, this guy now gets taxed at two, tagging on an additional 10 points. I continue to be baffled.
  • Brother-Captain: +5pts. This is a small change, but is sort of a head-scratcher. Maybe it is just accounting for the psycannon you are for sure taking? I am stumped, the Brother-Captain isn’t really a great option, so the point change is weird.
  • Castellan Crowe/Purifiers: +10 points for Crowe and +5/10pts for Purifiers. This is yet another weird nerf, as the main benefit of Purifiers is their Anti-Infantry and Ignore Cover brain bullets, which Crowe boosts. However, Ignore Cover and Psychic basically do the same thing in most situations, meaning this common unit just sort of caught strays for no reason.
  • Grand Master Voldus: +30pts. This one at least I get. Voldus gives a -1 to Hit roll, which does stack with the Benefit of Cover now. -2 to Hit can be absolutely brutal, and makes Voldus a very interesting option for a large Terminator unit of some variety. On first blush, I still think a 30 point hike is a bit steep, though, so I’m throwing it in the “Nerf” category. However, this doesn’t mean that Voldus isn’t potentially quite good.

Outlook

Loser

Thoughts

Garrett: Grey Knights have not been on the top of their game in recent months, and I suspect these changes don’t help that. Taxes on multiple Dreadknights, essentially no buffs throughout the book, and the changes to the core game with things like cover leave the GK in a rough spot. The bright side is they have some solid new tools and decent access to different kinds of dispositions, so I am hopeful that I am wrong and that the knights of Titan will come out swinging.

Imperial Knights

Imperial Knights suffered from the success of their Traitor cousins in 10th, as their Codex arrived a little later after the studio had time to figure out how to cost them. They’ve still had bouts of decent play since, and in late 10th some Freeblade Company lists have done well.

Key Points Changes

Buffs

  • Dominus Knights: -10 Pts. This is mostly a nice gesture since both these Knights suffer from the new cover changes but it won’t really get you to squeeze these into your lists. That said with the new detachment, these become kinda neat.
  • Knight Defender: -15 Pts. Another Knight that becomes somewhat worse with new cover. That said it also benefits from new cover a lot so this is a nice bump to go along with it.
  • Knight Castigator/Knight Acheron: -15 Pts. The bad Cerastus Knights go down because it turns out they’re not as good as the good ones, who knew.

Nerfs

  • Knight Lancer: +20 or +40 for your 2nd and 3rd. Lancers get a meaningful cover benefit now which makes them even more annoying to shift so it makes sense these go up.
  • Armiger Helverin: +5 Pts. This makes sense since AP-1 matters way more now.
  • Knight Crusader: +10 Pts. This is a bit of a headscratcher since this guy generally wants to be far away and shooting, an archetype thats gotten worse. Getting up close for plunging fire generally means getting charged and being -2 to hit a lot of targets. Maybe this is accounting for Mysterious Guardian?
  • Knight Destrier: +15 Pts. Ap-1 guns on a really fast platform that ignores cover? Yeah this was always gonna catch this nerf, although 15 is a lot.

Outlook

Neutral

Thoughts

Norman: Freeblade getting Purge and Destriers being posed for a strong place with the new cover rules gives Imperial Knights a strong start to the edition. The new cover rules really hurt a lot of the long range gameplan though, so tactics involving pivoting around home to shoot your opponents across the map probably won’t work as well. Imperial Knights weren’t exactly lighting the world on fire before 11th though, and we don’t see this shifting too much.
