[40K] 11th Edition Force Disposition Review: Purge the Foe

by Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones, Shane Watts | Jun 03 2026
Welcome to Tabletop Battles’ coverage of Purge the Foe missions! In this article, we’ll look at each of the five possible missions that a Purge the Foe army will be confronted with. We’ll explain how to score primaries for each objective in the missions, break down how your opponent will score, and then give tips on offense, defense, and listbuilding (if applicable). Finally, we'll rate each mission on a scale of 1 to 5 to give an idea of the relative strength of the mission compared to their opponent’s mission. At the end, we’ll average out those results to give the whole Force Disposition a general strength rating.

Purge the Foe is tied with Take and Hold as the most straightforward of the Force Dispositions, giving you missions which reward you for holding objectives (usually one outside your deployment zone) and killing enemy units. Those are two things your army is already going to be set up to do, and what the vast majority of units in the game are costed around. You may still want an action unit for scoring things like Cleanse, but if you’re taking Purge, it’s because you want to be rewarded for killing enemy units.

And that’s a huge benefit, because it’s not really possible to win a game of Warhammer 40k without holding objectives or killing enemy units, so having a mission that rewards you for doing that is pretty much always going to be good, and allows you to go all-in on your army’s most destructive units and rules. That said, the nuances of how you actually score vary by mission and your opponent’s disposition, so let’s dive into the specific missions for this Disposition.

In brackets at the end of each objective description you’ll see information detailing which battle rounds the objective is scored in and when in the turn they are scored. As always, Primary objectives are capped at 45 VP, and you can score a maximum of 15 VP per turn.

To evaluate matchups, we are scoring the matchup on a scale of 1-5 with the following criteria:

  • 1 - Terrible. This is a very bad matchup. The player has a big deficit in the ease of scoring primaries to overcome.
  • 2 - Disadvantaged. This is a bad matchup. The player has a slightly harder time scoring maximum primary points.
  • 3 - Even. This is an even matchup. Either both players have the same mission (mirror match), or are roughly equal with regard to ease of scoring primaries.
  • 4 - Advantaged. This is a good matchup. The player has a slightly easier time scoring max primaries.
  • 5 - Great. This is a very good matchup. The player has an extremely favorable set of objectives relative to their opponent.

Finally, as we go through the mission descriptions, take note of the number of objectives attached to each one. It will be either five or six, and what those numbers really mean is if there is a big central terrain area objective or not. If it’s five, then there will be a big central objective, and if it’s six, then that objective will be broken up into 2 smaller, triangular central objectives. Having one big objective in the middle of the table really impacts pace, flow, and scoring during a game, so keep an eye out - it tends to make things deadlier, and that will most often benefit the Purge player.

But before we do that, we’d like to thank Games Workshop for sending us a preview copy of these rules for Review Purposes.

Unstoppable Force (vs. Take and Hold) 

Purge the Foe vs. Take and Hold

There are three layout options for this matchup, and all three of them use a single central objective - that’s terrible news for the Take and Hold player, who needs to control a central objective to score 3 VP each round and only scores for holding objectives outside their Deployment Zone. 

How You Score

Unstoppable Force plays how you would think. You score Primary points every turn if you kill one or more enemy units. Starting on the second Battle Round, you’ll also score points in your command phase for each objective you control that isn’t your home objective, and at the end of your turn if you control an objective you didn’t control at the start of your turn you’ll score another 3 VP. Finally you can score an extra 5 bonus VP at the end of the battle for holding a Central Objective.

This mission encourages you to actively push your Take and Hold opponent off the objectives and kill them, and to ensure they never, ever hold that Central objective on their turn. Ideally you want to ensure you are holding your expansion objective, kill an enemy unit every turn, and starting turn 2 have a unit that can take an objective you didn’t have which is more than likely the center. The fun part here is that you don’t have to grab an objective an opponent controls, just one you didn’t, so if you get shot off your Expansion on turn 1 well, good news - it’s worth 3 VP when you walk back onto it on your turn. 

If all you do is kill one, hold one, and walk onto one each turn you’ll score 10 VP each on rounds 2 to 5 for an easy 40/45 VP, and a Kill turn 1 puts you ahead of that clock. Keep your opponent off the center objective in their Command phase and you’ll do just fine - you can just keep trading the center all game, because every time you walk onto it you’ll score 3 VP and deny your opponent 7.

How Your Opponent Scores

Your opponent is going to score on what–you guessed it, holding objectives. At the end of any turn they’ll score 3 VP if they control a central objective. Then, in rounds 2 through 4 they’ll score 5 VP in their Command phase for each objective they control that isn’t their home, and score that at the end of the turn in round 5. Scoring 3 VP for walking onto the central objective can be pretty easy - if the Take and Hold player can hold their expansion all game and manage to snag the Center objective every turn they can max primary, but if they end up trading the center all game, they’re more likely to be looking at 35 VP for primary as they continually walk onto the center objective and let the Purge player score 3 for retaking it.

Tips for Offense

If you have an opportunity to hold an extra objective into your turn, or have assets to lock in holding the center objective at the end of the game, you’ll get your 44/45 points locked in, assuming you follow the above scoring metric. While holding the center objective is crucial, it’s much less important for the Purge player - you can afford to wipe it clean, walk onto it for a quick score with a chaff unit, then lose that unit and start over.

Tips for Defense

The best way to deny your opponent primary points mostly aligns with 10th ed mindset: Stop them from getting onto objectives. If you can make your opponent unable to move onto the center objective on their turn, they are losing points, every turn you can pull this off. Keep in mind that your opponent can stop you with this method too. So maintaining control of the center objective is critical. 

Tips for Listbuilding

Your lists here will look similar to tenth edition lists; you’ll need disposable units to take objectives and walk on and score on your turn, melee units to flip objectives, and killy ranged units which can clear an opponent on an objective to prevent them from scoring. This might be the most straight forward mission that Purge the Foe plays.

Matchup Evaluation

5 - Both the scoring and the layouts in this mission favor the Purge player, and if we don’t give this one a 5, we’re not going to give any matchup a 5. At best, your opponent can try to keep pace.

Meatgrinder (vs Purge the Foe) 

Purge the Foe vs. Purge the Foe

Welcome to classic Purge the Foe for our Purge mirror match. This one’s all about killing and killing more. All three of the these layouts feature split central objectives, and two of them have the extra wrinkle that they only have two objectives outside of your Deployment Zone (though you still only have one home objective), making holding one objective that isn’t your home trivially easy.

How You Score

I have good news: Again you’ll score points by killing 1+ enemy units every turn. Starting turn two you’ll score points in your command phase if you hold 1+ objective that isn’t your home. Additionally at the end of your turn you’ll score points if you killed more enemy units than your opponent did in their last turn, and more points if you control their home objective. With this being a mirror match the flight path is a little more unclear, if you kill a unit every turn and turn 2+ hold your expansion, you should expect to score 31 points. So you really have to bank on killing more than your opponent or reliably taking their home objective.

How Your Opponent Scores

Well now I have the bad news, your opponent scores exactly the same way you do. So it becomes a game of who can capitalize.

Tips for Offense

Ok so this is the sauce, you want to cap that score? Annihilate your opponent, preferably stealing their home objective as soon as possible. Neither of those things are exactly easy, but if you can read the matrix and see the path to taking their home, you are well on your way.

Tips for Defense

We already mentioned this is a game of capitalizing on your opponent’s mistakes, but if you want to deny them points, you want to give them the least amount of kills possible and protect your home. If you know you have to risk more units of yours to kill a unit of theirs, you are going to give up kill more, so don’t do it. This is THE cat and mouse game, be the cat.

Tips for Listbuilding

You do not want to sacrifice units, unless it requires your opponent to over commit. Giving up free kills is a bad deal here, since those are great fodder for “Kill more.” Durable units (or units capable of mitigating what can hit them) are going to be at a premium in this fight. Of course you will have to have your powerhouse units that sweep your opponent off the field, but if you don’t have these, are you really playing Purge the Foe or is it just sparkling Take and Hold?

Matchup Evaluation

3 - BE THE CAT, don’t get got. Otherwise you lose. Going second tends to be better here as it allows you to react to your opponent when it comes to scoring kill more.

Consecrate (vs Reconnaissance) 

Purge the Foe vs Recon

This mission splits the center objective in every layout, and puts four objectives in no man’s land on each of its three layouts. That makes for some fairly obscured middle zones, though you’ll still find there are long firing lanes down the sides of the table on each layout. This makes holding one or more objectives fairly easy.

How You Score

Ready for a weird one? We have to start with a mechanic, and then we can talk scoring. Each time one of your units destroys an enemy unit, they become a Consecration unit. At the end of your turn, each Consecration unit that is in range of an objective that isn’t your home objective or an objective that has been Consecrated already can place a marker on that objective. That objective is now Consecrated and your Consecration unit is no longer a Consecration unit. So basically, you kill stuff and get Khorne’s blessing, then pass it along to an objective, repeat.

Now that that’s explained, here’s the scoring stuff: At the end of every turn you score points for the number of Consecrated objectives. If only one or two objectives have been consecrated  you’ll score 3 VP, while if you’ve consecrated three or more you’ll net 6 VP. Starting turn 2 each Command phase (or end of turn on the final battle round), you’ll score 4 VP if you control one or more objectives outside your Home and another 4 VP if you control more objectives than your opponent.

Finally at the end of the game if you Consecrate an opponent’s home objective you get an extra 5 VP.

How Your Opponent Scores

Your Recon opponent has to rely on doing actions on the objectives to score most of their points. This action is called Triangulation. Starting on the second Battle Round, Triangulation can be done once per turn on an objective that is not their home objective, and completes at the end of turn, provided that the Recon player controls that objective. Once an objective has been Triangulated they will place a marker on that objective.

Starting in the second battle round, the Recon player will score 4 VP in their Command phase if they control at least one objective that isn’t their home objective. Then at the end of their turn they’ll score 3/6/10 VP if they’ve Triangulated one, two, or three objectives. At the end of the battle they can score 10 VP if they control four or more objectives. 

Assuming the Recon player is able to hold their expansion starting turn 2 and Triangulate an objective on each of turns 2 and 3, they will score 37 points, which gives them an edge on the passive game.

Tips for Offense

If you want to max your points, you are gonna need to have some mobility, or at least capability of leaping onto your opponent’s objectives. The faster you can get to three objectives Consecrated the better; you could achieve this by turn 2 or 3 against an opponent also actively trying to Triangulate objectives, and you have the benefit of being able to consecrate three objectives in the first battle round if you can set your units up well. If your opponent leaves their backfield undefended - which is hard to do with the Hammer and Anvil layout here - you can snag a good chance to score the bonus.

Tips for Defense

The good news is that your offensive and defensive strategies tend to be the same: Kill your opponent and stop them from walking onto center objectives, using your 2” Engagement Range to block their movement and prevent them from walking on in the Movement phase. Failing that, just put OC on objectives such that an opponent can’t complete the action, or be ready to charge in with Heroic Intervention to stop them. 

Tips for Listbuilding 

Speed is going to be king for this match up. Ideally you want to include a fast melee unit that reliably get a kill and end the turn on an objective; note that you don’t have to control it to Consecrate it, just have a Consecration unit on it. Also a fast shooty unit works the same way, move onto the objective and kill a unit, done. Ideally you do both in the same turn. Also you could have a unit on your expansion objective that shoots an enemy unit to death and snag one that way. So just pay attention to where you can snag “instant” consecration, via getting the kill and already being on the objective.

Matchup Evaluation

3 - This one is tough to evaluate. It’s not a mission that the Purge player can play defensively - they’ll lose on primary if the opponent triangulates three objectives. It's not entirely easy for the Recon player, either, because they can only Triangulate one objective per turn. The secret weapon in the Purge player’s arsenal is Heroic Intervention here.

Destroyer’s Wrath (vs Priority Assets) 

Purge the Foe vs. Priority Assets

This mission features three layouts with split central objectives, and all three layouts put a second objective in each player’s deployment zone, meaning the two central objectives are the only ones in No Man’s Land. 

How You Score

Did you think you were gonna score points by killing units? Well, good news - it’s true here too. At the end of every turn, if you destroyed an enemy unit, you’ll score 3 VP. Starting in the second round, in your command phase (end of turn on turn on round 5), you’ll score points for holding 1+ objectives that aren’t your home, with bonus points if you hold more than your opponent. As we mentioned, there are going to be two objectives in your deployment zone, so holding one non-home is easy; but that’ll be true for your opponent too - you’ll need to hold one center objective and keep the opponent off the other to score hold more. 

Additionally you’ll score points if you killed more enemy units than your opponent did in their previous turn. This is the true Purge the Foe reboot from 10th ed. The good news here is that unlike in tenth edition, scoring is based on prior turn kills, not the round, so you’ll always know your target number of kills - a big help. If you get your first kill on round 2 and hold your expansion every round you’ll get a passive score of 28 VP.

How Your Opponent Scores

For those of you who don’t know yet, Priority Assets is like THE action disposition. Their points are gonna ramp based on doing the Vital Link action. Once per turn they can do an action on one of the central objectives, which completes at the end of their turn if they control the objective. If successful they place a marker on the objective. Note that they can keep doing this on the same objective, dropping more tokens on that objective each round. That’s important, because at the end of every turn score points for controlling 1+ center objectives (not expansion), and bonus points for each marker they have on those center objectives they control. Specifically 1 VP for each. Which means if they do the action on turns 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5, they’ll score an extra 10 VP from cumulative scoring.

Starting in Round 2 the Priority Assets player will also score points for holding one or more objectives that aren’t their home, with bonus 4 VP if what they hold is a center objective. Additionally they will score a whopping 10 points if they control your home objective at the end of the game. Assuming they control one center objective and action it by the end of their turn every turn and hold their expansion starting turn 2, they will score 41 points. Yikes.

Tips for Offense

The Purge player has to be aggressive - the name of the game is keeping the Priority Assets player from scoring their bonus 4 VP for holding a center objective in their Command phase. That’s easy to do on Rounds 2/3/4, and if you can manage that you’ll deny them 12 VP and cap their scoring at something more like 39 VP.  At the same time you’ll need to avoid giving up easy kills and keeping track of your opponent’s kills so you can snag Kill More at least twice.

Tips for Defense

This is gonna sound a lot like the advice about the Recon match up. You have to stop them from doing the action, and the best way to do that is by blocking them off the terrain and having a unit which can perform a Heroic Intervention into an action unit if they try - note that’ll cost you 2 CP, so plan to have lots of CP on hand, or a way to reduce costs. 

Tips for Listbuilding

You want units which can effectively perform a Heroic Intervention, ideally at a reduced cost. Having sacrificial units which can occupy a central objective and move-block Priority Assets action units is also huge, as it’ll ensure they can’t walk onto the objective to actually do the action. 

Matchup Evaluation

2.5 - We’ve had some heated discussions about whether the Priority Assets player is favored here, and it’ll depend heavily on the matchup. If the Purge player has easy access to move-blocking or Heroic Intervention, they’ll have an easier time preventing the Priority player from scoring, and if they can stop just one token from dropping early it’ll be a 4+ point swing. The big factor here is the layouts - if the layouts for this one had a single central objective, this would be a 3 or even a 3.5.

Punishment (vs Disruption) 

Purge the Foe vs. Disruption

All three of these layouts have three central objectives, and all three put them in no man’s land. This one has an extra wrinkle, in that at the start of each of their turns, the Purge player will choose three Disruption units which are on the battlefield and in range of an objective marker and/or destroyed a friendly unit in the previous turn. If there are none, then the Purge player just picks one unit. The chosen units are Condemned.

How You Score

Those Condemned units matter - at the end of any turn, if one or more Condemned units left the battlefield that turn, you score 5 VP. Note that the clause here isn’t destroyed, it’s leaves the battlefield - this is a great way to disrupt your opponent’s plans to pull that Callidus Assassin or Scout Squad up into reserves (get REKT Grey Knights), by making them pay you 5 VP for the privilege. You can be strategic with this one.

From the second round onwards, at the end of your Command phase (or end of your turn in round 5), you’ll score 4 VP if you control one or more objectives that isn’t your home, and 5 VP if you control more objectives than the opponent.

At the end of the battle you’ll score 8 VP for controlling your opponent’s home objective.

If you hold your expansion turn 2 onward and a Condemned unit leaves the table (preferably via death) for 3 turns you are looking at 31 points. Pretty good for mostly “passive” gameplay.

How Your Opponent Scores

The Disruption player has similar goals, but with some caveats - at the end of their turn, they’ll score 2 VP for each enemy unit they destroyed that turn. Starting in the second battle round, in their Command phase (or end of turn in round 5), they’ll score 4 VP if they control one or more objectives that isn’t their home, and 3 VP at the end of their turn if they control one or more central objectives and one or more expansion objectives. That’s a tall order when you remember that there are only three midtable objectives on these layouts. 

Hold one is easy and walking onto home + center for 3VP means the Disruption player can score 7 points per turn pretty easily here, with another 2 to 6 on kills. That all adds up to about 38 points from mostly passive scoring, and means that the Purge player has to be aggressive on this one to prevent an opponent from holding the central objective.

Tips for Offense

You need to be strategic with your choices for Condemned units. You likely won’t be able to score more than 5 VP off primary on the first round unless you are either going second or your opponent is really dumb and sets you up with free infiltrators on objectives, so your focus should be killing on condemned unit and scoring hold more, since that’ll net you 14 VP per turn and is  key to preventing your opponent from scoring their bonus 3 VP.

Tips for Defense

It’s hard to stop your opponent from walking onto the center objective if they have melee units, but having something big and heavy on OC to defend it is a solid play here. Your big challenge here is that you can absolutely just give up a bunch of kill points to the Disruption player if you aren’t careful, so you want to avoid giving up free kills of your own - though the kill math tends to favor the Purge player.

Tips for Listbuilding

The name of the game here is not giving up free kills, same as we’ve seen with several other Purge missions. 

Matchup Evaluation

3.5 - This would be a 3 if it weren’t for the fact that those layouts favor Purge. Disruption has a solid passive game into Purge here, but Purge will have an easier time maxing primary, and purge turns are more likely to be 14 VP to 11-13 for Disruption.

Final Thoughts

Overall strength of Force Disposition: 3.6

The general consensus in the Tabletop Battles offices is that Purge the Foe is the strongest of the Dispositions - it rewards you for doing things you already wanted to do, and having layouts with a single central objective tends to benefit its game plan and scoring. The one matchup we think it may struggle into is against Priority Assets, but that’s something you can plan for explicitly and build around - and may not matter if the top armies are all on Purge.
