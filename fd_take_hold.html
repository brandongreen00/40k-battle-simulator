[40k] 11th Edition Force Disposition Review: Take and Hold

by Dan "dittka" Boyd | Jun 02 2026
Welcome to Tabletop Battles’ coverage of Take and Hold missions! In this article, we’ll look at each of the five possible missions that a Take and Hold army will be confronted with. We’ll explain how to score primaries for each objective in the missions, break down how your opponent will score, and then give tips on offense, defense, and listbuilding (if applicable). Finally, we'll rate each mission on a scale of 1 to 5 to give an idea of the relative strength of the mission compared to their opponent’s mission. At the end, we’ll average out those results to give the whole Force Disposition a general strength rating.

Do you like standing around on objectives? Do you think performing actions is too much of a mental drain? Are you 9 years old? If you said yes to any of this, then do I have the Force Disposition for you: Take and Hold! Enjoy scintillating gameplay actions such as: Moving onto an objective, trying not to get shot off said objective, and then repeating the process ad nauseum! Truly the pinnacle of strategic wargaming.

Sarcasm aside, the Take and Hold (TH) Force Disposition is an easily digestible and consistent set of mission objectives that just about any army can make use of. If your units are tough or you’ve brought a lot of them, TH missions will reward you with plenty of primary VPs without asking you to think too hard about it.

In brackets at the end of each objective description you’ll see information detailing which battle rounds the objective is scored in and when in the turn they are scored. As always, Primary objectives are capped at 45 VP, and you can score a maximum of 15 VP per turn.

To evaluate matchups, we are scoring the matchup on a scale of 1-5 with the following criteria:

  • 1 - Terrible. This is a very bad matchup. The player has a big deficit in the ease of scoring primaries to overcome.
  • 2 - Disadvantaged. This is a bad matchup. The player has a slightly harder time scoring maximum primary points.
  • 3 - Even. This is an even matchup. Either both players have the same mission (mirror match), or are roughly equal with regard to ease of scoring primaries.
  • 4 - Advantaged. This is a good matchup. The player has a slightly easier time scoring max primaries.
  • 5 - Great. This is a very good matchup. The player has an extremely favorable set of objectives relative to their opponent.

Finally, as we go through the mission descriptions, take note of the number of objectives attached to each one. It will be either five or six, and what those numbers really tell you is if there is a big central terrain area objective or not. If it’s five, then there will be a big central objective, and if it’s six, then that objective will be broken up into two smaller, triangular central objectives. Having one big objective in the middle of the table really impacts pace, flow, and scoring during a game, so keep an eye out - having two middle objectives makes things a bit safer, and will tend to benefit the Take and Hold Player.

But before we do that, we’d like to thank Games Workshop for sending us a preview copy of these rules for Review Purposes.

First up: The mirror match!

Battlefield Dominance (vs Take and Hold) [5 Objectives]

Take and Hold mirror match

How You Score

You’ve got two ways to score primary VPs in this mission:

  1. Score 2 VP if you control more objectives than your opponent. [1st and 2nd battle rounds, end of your turn]
  2. Score 3 VP for each objective you control (+2VP for each objective excluding your home objective, if you control your home objective). [2nd battle round onwards, end of your command phase, changes to end of turn in the 5th battle round]

Pretty straightforward! Let’s see what scoring will probably look like through the course of a game:

  • Turn 1 - 2 (control more at end of turn)
  • Turn 2 - 10 (home objective, close expansion objective, central objective at end of turn for 3 out of 5)
  • Turn 3 - 10
  • Turn 4 - 10
  • Turn 5 - 15 (since scoring objective 2 changes to end of turn, here, you should be able to jump on a third objective at the end of your turn)

This example assumes that the central objective will change hands every turn allowing for the 2 VP at end of turn, but not sticking around to score in the next friendly command phase.

How Your Opponent Scores

Since this is a mirror match, your opponent will be playing the same mission as you, and will thus score in the exact same manner!

Tips for Offense

Pushing out to snag the central objective or even going hard into their territory is imperative to max primary, because if you don’t, you’ll be stuck scoring 8 per turn which will leave you at a measly 32 VPs. And if you wait until T5 to make the big push, you’re going to run into the per turn scoring cap and only score 39 VP, so you’ve got to balance controlling the middle and not getting your whole army shot off the board too early. Remember: scoring is at the end of turn on T5, so have some units left over to hop on objectives to max it out if you haven’t yet scored 45!

Having units to capture the far expansion objective or even the opponent’s home for even one turn can swing the game in your favor, so make sure to look out for opportunities to do just that!

Tips for Defense

Since this is a mirror match, your offense and defense kind of line up together. Once again, you’ve got to play cagey and not let your whole army get murked early on. As soon as your units start standing on the center objective, they’re going to have enormous targets painted on them, so make sure they’re super tough or you’ve got plenty of spares.

Tips for Listbuilding

Your scoring units in the center are probably going to die, so you might want to think about bringing a whole lot of MSUs with high OC to keep the scoring going. Also, infiltrating units to give you a T1 edge or even to disrupt opponent T2 scoring might be a good shout.

Matchup Evaluation

This is a mirror match, so it’s fully even.

Evaluation Score: 3

Determined Acquisition (vs Disruption) [5 Objectives]

Take and Hold vs. Disruption

How You Score

There are two ways to score in this mission:

  1. Score 2 VP for each objective you control that you did not control at the start of the turn, excluding home objective. [Any battle round, end of your turn]
  2. Score 3 VP for each objective your control (+3 VP if that objective is within your opponent’s territory). [2nd battle round onwards, end of your command phase, changes to end of turn in the 5th battle round]
    • It is important to note that your home objective counts for this, as the Disruption player does not get to score their home. Don’t get confused!
    • It is also important to note that the big, central objective is within your opponent’s territory, so holding on to it is worth 6 VP per turn!

Once again it’s pretty straightforward. Push up towards the middle and try not to let your opponent eat all of your units. Here’s what scoring will probably look like:

  • Turn 1 - 4 VP (2 each for the close expansion and central objectives)
  • Turn 2 - 8 VP if you score home, close expansion, and have central at end of turn, but it jumps up to 14 VP if you could have held on to the central from last turn.
  • Turn 3 - 8 to 14 VP
  • Turn 4 - 8 to 14 VP
  • Turn 5 - 14 to 15 VP

Without getting the bonus points for controlling objectives in enemy territory all game and only re-capping the central objective every turn, you’re going to net 36 VP. Which means that you only need to hold on to an objective within the enemy territory 3 times during the game to max primary. And one of those instances can be done on T5, when it’s at the end of the turn! It might not be automatic to max primary, but it’s pretty dang close.

How Your Opponent Scores (Death Trap)

Take and Hold players could never with this set of objectives. There are 3 ways for the Disruption player to score:

1. Score 2VP per terrain area trapped this turn (+3 pts if the terrain area is also an objective). [Any battle round, end of their turn]

Action - Booby Trap: 
  • Starts: Their shooting phase.
  • Eligible units: One enemy unit within range of one objective that is not their home objective OR within one terrain area that is not in their deployment zone that is not yet trapped.
  • Use limit: Unlimited. Each unit must be within a different terrain area.
  • Completes: Immediately.
  • Effect: The terrain area is trapped. Place an operation marker within that terrain area.

An important thing to note here: They do NOT have to control an objective to trap it, and they can trap a terrain area with enemy units on it. Their unit just has to touch the target terrain area, and only that terrain area, to trap it.

The trapped condition does not expire or get removed regardless of how many of your units are within the trapped terrain area.

2. Score 3VP if 1 or more enemy units were destroyed while within a trapped terrain area this turn. [Any battle round, end of their turn]

3. Score 4VP if they control 1 or more objectives, excluding their home objective [2nd battle round onwards, end of their command phase, changes to end of their turn in battle round 5]

I know, Take and Hold players, this hurts to look at. It’s okay, daddy’s gonna tell you how it will pan out. Here’s what scoring will look like without counting points for killing units:

  • Turn 1 - 12 VP (trap the far expansion, the center, and the far NML ruins. Could be 14 if they want to trap the close NML ruins)
  • Turn 2 - 11 VP (trap the close expansion, the close NML ruins, score 4 for the close expansion)
  • Turn 3 - 4 VP (controlling close expansion)
  • Turn 4 - 4 VP
  • Turn 5 - 4 VP

Before killing any enemy units, the Disruption player can net 35 VP in primary points. They’ll need a bunch of infiltrators or extremely fast units to do it, but that isn’t really a problem for most armies. So, to max out primary, they’ll need to kill 4 of your units standing in trapped terrain areas and/or sneak into your backfield and trap stuff in your territory. Not particularly difficult to do, especially if they bring deep strikers and fast infantry.

Tips for Offense

Which layout you get is going to be a huge factor with this mission. In the Hammer and Anvil layout, the Disruption player has a lot of wiggle room in that huge backfield to sneak in and start trapping your stuff. In the Sweeping Engagement layout, you both are going to end up forting up on the same side, and since the Disruption player doesn’t need to score their home, you might be able to swoop in and snatch it. 

Once again, the center objective is within the enemy’s territory, so holding it can net you a ton of points. The only issue is that the Disruption player not only knows this but can actively benefit from it and will go all out to knock out your scoring units standing on that objective. So you will kinda need to drip feed scoring units into the center and be poised to go after the far expansion or even the enemy home at a moment’s notice.

Tips for Defense

First off, stay the hell away from any trapped terrain areas that aren’t objectives. You don’t need to touch them, so never touch them. If you don’t have a bunch of infiltrators, then the Disruption player's gonna go hog wild with scoring on T1. If you do have infiltrators, though, go ahead and get yours ready to charge their T1 if you go 1st. If they Fall Back, they can’t perform an action, and you might be able to stifle their scoring. Finally, look for opportunities to break their grip on the far expansion objective, as it will probably be where they fort up.

Matchup Evaluation

This one’s pretty close! You have the slight edge in default primary scoring, but the Disruption player has a lot more agency and reactivity in how they score points, especially in the Hammer and Anvil or Crucible of Battle deployment layouts. So all in all, I’d say this one’s evenly matched.

Evaluation Score: 3

Immovable Object (vs Purge the Foe) [5 Objectives]

Purge the Foe vs. Take and Hold

How You Score

There are 3 ways to score in this mission:

  1. Score 3 VP if you control 1 or more central objectives. [Any battle round, end of your turn]
  2. Score 5 VP for each objective you control, excluding home. [2nd to 4th battle rounds, end of your command phase]
  3. Score 5 VP for each objective you control, excluding home. [5th battle round, end of turn]

Once again, we’re standing on objectives. Let’s look at scoring:

  • Turn 1 - 3 VP
  • Turn 2 - 8 VP (re-take center, hold expansion)
  • Turn 3 - 8 VP
  • Turn 4 - 8 VP
  • Turn 5 - 12-15 VP

You’re looking at 33-36 VP without over-extending by too much and holding back for a big T5. That’s not great! To max primary, you’re going to have to attempt to have at least two turns of holding 2 objectives, and the Purge player is definitely not going to want to let you do that. You’re also running into a problem where even just sitting back and waiting for a big turn is going to mean you’re drip-feeding scoring units, which the Purge player is going to absolutely love. Speaking of which:

How Your Opponent Scores (Unstoppable Force)

A whopping 4 ways to score for the Purge player:

  1. Score 3 VP if one or more enemy units were destroyed this turn. [Any battle round, end of their turn]
  2. Score 4 VP for each objective they control, excluding home. [2nd battle round onwards, end of your command phase, changes to end of turn in the 5th battle round]
  3. Score 3 VP if they control one or more objectives they did not control at start of turn, excluding home. [2nd battle round onwards, end of turn]
  4. Score 5 VP if they control one or more central objectives. [End of battle]

Lots of ways to score! Let’s see how it will look:

  • Turn 1 - 3 VP (kill something)
  • Turn 2 - 10 VP (control 1, kill something, take a new objective)
  • Turn 3 - 7-11 VP(mix of controlling 1 or 2, killing something, grabbing a new objective)
  • Turn 4 - 7-11 VP
  • Turn 5 - 12-15 VP

On the low end, the Purge player is looking at a pretty easy 39, and if they stretch they can definitely max primary. Not ideal!

Tips for Offense

I hate to spoil the evaluation, but this is a bad matchup. You’ll have to be aggressive if you want to max primary, and that can easily play into the Purge player’s hands. If you’re gonna win on primary, you’ll need to have at least 2 turns of controlling 2 NML objectives in the command phase, and to offset the probable 5 VP at the end of the game that the Purge player will score, you’ll need to take the center objective twice without giving up any kill points, and that’s going to be hard. Not impossible, but hard.

Tips for Defense

Set up so that if you go 2nd, the Purge player can’t kill anything T1. And if you go 1st, you’ve got to make it as hard as possible for them to get T1 kill points. After that, you need to be cagey and careful. This is a good game for strat reserves and Rapid Ingress.

Matchup Evaluation

Oof. This is an uphill climb for sure. Not quite the worst matchup I’ve seen, but definitely not a good one.

Evaluation Score: 1.5

Inescapable Dominion (vs Priority Assets) [6 Objectives]

Take and Hold vs. Priority Assets

Finally, a six-objective game for the standing around disposition! Let’s see if we’ve really reached the promised land:

How You Score

There are four ways to score in this mission:

  1. Score 4 VP if you control 3 or more objectives. [Any battle round, end of your turn]
  2. Score 5 VP if you control 2 or more objectives. [2nd battle round onwards, end of your command phase, changes to end of turn in the 5th battle round]
  3. Score 4 VP if you control more objectives than your opponent. [2nd battle round onwards, end of your command phase, changes to end of turn in the 5th battle round]
  4. Score 5 VP if you control the enemy’s home objective. [End of battle]

Looks pretty good off the bat, let’s see where it lands:

  • Turn 1 - 4 VP (stand on 3)
  • Turn 2 - 9 VP (hold 2+, stand on 3)
  • Turn 3 - 9 VP
  • Turn 4 - 9 VP
  • Turn 5 - 13 VP (hold 2+, stand on 3, have more)

Now we’re talking! That’s 44 with conservative play and a T5 push. Not bad at all! A cheeky back-cap or one turn of aggression will easily net you maximum primary.

How Your Opponent Scores (Secure Asset)

The Priority Asset (PA) player has 4 ways to score:

  1. Score 4 VP if one of their units has secured the asset this turn. [Any battle round, end of turn]

    • Action - Secure Asset

      • Starts: Their shooting phase.
      • Eligible units: One enemy unit within range of one objective, excluding home.
      • Use limit: Once per turn.
      • Completes: End of their turn, if they control the objective.
      • Effect: Their unit secures the asset.
  2. Score 2 VP if one or more enemy units within range of one or more central objectives are destroyed. [Any battle round, end of turn]
  3. Score 4 VP if they control one or more objectives, excluding home. [2nd battle round onwards, end of your command phase, changes to end of turn in the 5th battle round]
  4. Score 4 VP if they control 3 or more objectives. [2nd battle round onwards, end of your command phase, changes to end of turn in the 5th battle round]

Once again, a 4-ways-to-score mission always looks fresh. Let’s see if it stays fresh:

  • Turn 1 - 4-6 (secured asset, maybe kill one unit in the center)
  • Turn 2 - 14 (secured asset, kill a unit in the center, hold 1, hold 3)
  • Turn 3 - 14
  • Turn 4 - 14
  • Turn 5 - 14

Hachi machi, that’s a lot of points. Even if the PA player misses a turn of killing a unit in the center or holding 3 objectives, they can make it up with room to spare.

Tips for Offense

If you can sit on 3 objectives on a 6-objective layout for 5 turns, you can easily get 44, and if you stretch just a little you can get 45. This one’s pretty easy. Since the PA player is going to want to get kill points, you can play defensively and drip feed MSUs into the center. Wait for them to over-extend and capitalize with a rapid ingress or with some cleverly staged units.

Tips for Defense

Stopping the PA player from scoring a million points is going to be hard. This mission seems really, really easy for them. Stopping them from getting T1 kill points will be a start, but after that, you need to be careful to not over-extend too early.

Matchup Evaluation

I really, really want to say it’s even here, but the PA player has so many ways to easily score points that I think the slight edge goes to them, even though the TH player can basically do nothing and score 44 VP. Have fun with secondaries, you two!

Evaluation Score: 2.5

Purge and Secure (vs Reconnaissance) [5 Objectives]

Take and Hold vs. Reconnaissance

Last one! Let’s hope it’s a good one! All three of these layouts are five-objective maps. 

How You Score

There are three ways to score in this mission:

  1. Score 3 VP if one or more enemy units were destroyed this turn by a unit that was within range of one or more objectives OR one or more enemy units that started the turn within range of one or more objectives were destroyed this turn. [Any battle round, end of your turn]
  2. Score 4 VP for each objective you control, excluding home. [2nd battle round onwards, end of your command phase, changes to end of turn in the 5th battle round]
  3. Score 3 VP if you control one or more objectives that you did not control at the start of the turn, excluding home. [2nd battle round onwards, end of your turn]

Kill points are finally on the menu for the Take and Hold player! Let’s see what the points look like:

  • Turn 1 - 0-3 VP (if you go 1st, probably 0, if you go 2nd, probably 3)
  • Turn 2 - 10-14 (kill one, hold 1 or 2, take one new one)
  • Turn 3 - 10-14
  • Turn 4 - 10-14
  • Turn 5 - 14-15

Looking pretty good! Even if you hold 1 objective per turn while re-taking the center or the far expansion, if you can hold back for a big rush on T5, it’s looking very easy to max primary.

How Your Opponent Scores (Reconnaissance Sweep)

3 ways to score for the Recon player:

  1. Score 3 VP if three or more of their units are wholly within three different table quarters and not within 6” of the center of the battlefield OR Score 6 VP if four or more of their units are wholly within four different table quarters and not within 6” of the center of the battlefield. [Any battle round, end of their turn]
  2. Score 1 VP for each enemy unit destroyed this turn. [Any battle round, end of turn]
  3. Score 3 VP if they control 1 or more objectives, excluding home. [2nd battle round onwards, end of their command phase, changes to end of turn in the 5th battle round

Oh boy, Engage on All Fronts as a primary! You have to imagine the Recon player has built for this, so let’s see what we’ve got:

  • Turn 1 - 6 VP (4 quarters)
  • Turn 2 - 10 VP (4 quarters, 2 units killed, hold 1)
  • Turn 3 - 10 VP
  • Turn 4 - 10 VP
  • Turn 5 - 10 VP

Looks pretty good! Especially if they’ve built for spreading out. Which they probably will, because Take and Hold is surely going to be one of the more popular Force Dispositions.

Tips for Offense

This one’s pretty easy to get max primary, but make sure your damage dealers can go touch an objective real quick if they need to. Otherwise, play it cool and leap at the chance for a big T5.

Tips for Defense

Don’t feed them units! The Recon player has to interact with only 1 objective this game, so it might behoove you to let them have it and focus on spreading out to stop the consistent 6 VP/turn from allowing them to be in 4 quarters. But also, don’t feed the Recon player!

Matchup Evaluation

This one looks pretty even. The Recon player has a lot of agency, but the TH player can pretty much just sit around and score sky-high.

Evaluation Score: 3

Final Thoughts

Evaluation average: 2.6

Honestly, I was a little surprised by this score. I thought Take and Hold would do better, but the Purge matchup is atrocious, and the Priority Assets matchup is slightly disadvantageous. While the mental load might be lower for this Force Disposition, it seems to me that there are no real advantages to playing it when compared to the other Disposition’s missions. It’s probably a good set of missions for early 11th games, but it might end up lagging behind one or two of the other dispositions. 

I say no real advantages, but I just want to clarify something: in any matchup, except for Purge the Foe, Take and Hold players will find their games fundamentally competitive. For me, the missions are a little same-y, but games with Take and Hold are going to be fun, fair, and down to the wire, just how we all like them.

One note on listbuilding: 4 out of 5 of the missions here involve a big, central objective. Understand that as soon as your guys go to step on it, and they will have to if you want to score points, your opponent is going to do their level best to kill them. You will be rewarded if you use a “go wide” strategy with this disposition. Take lots of cheap scoring units so when they die, you’re not totally hamstrung.

As the week rolls on, we’ll be taking a look at all the rest of the mission packs, so make sure to compare evaluation scores as the articles drop. Thanks for reading, friends! Have fun with 11th!
