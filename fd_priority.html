[40K] 11th Edition Force Disposition Review: Priority Assets

by James "One_Wing" Grover, Robert "TheChirurgeon" Jones, Shane Watts | Jun 04 2026
Welcome to Tabletop Battles’ coverage of Priority Assets missions! In this article, we’ll look at each of the five possible missions that a Priority Assets army will be confronted with. We’ll explain how to score primaries for each objective in the missions, break down how your opponent will score, and then give tips on offense, defense, and listbuilding (if applicable). Finally, we'll rate each mission on a scale of 1 to 5 to give an idea of the relative strength of the mission compared to their opponent’s mission. At the end, we’ll average out those results to give the whole Force Disposition a general strength rating.

Priority Assets is an Action-heavy Disposition, with those Actions being fairly tightly focused on objectives, often Central Objectives or those within the Opponent’s Territory. Priority Assets will reward armies with a reliable pool of fast Action units that can sweep forward, lock in points, and then, well, don’t worry too much about what happens after that. The missions also need you to reliably hold onto your Expansion Objective, and some want you to be able to pick off an enemy unit per turn. Fast, lethal armies like Drukhari should excel at this, as if you can keep the opponent on the back foot, you should be able to rack up points while keeping the foe constrained. Just be aware of the risk of over-stretching your forces, especially if you draw Secondaries that also require Actions.

As with our other reviews, we'd like to thank Games Workshop for providing us with a preview copy of these rules for review purposes.

Secure Asset (vs Take and Hold)

Take and Hold vs. Priority Assets

This is a six-Objective mission played on Dawn of War, Hammer and Anvil, or Crucible of Battle. Having six objectives is a big help for the Take and Hold player here, as we’ll see when we get to scoring. 

How You Score

At the end of each of your turns, you score 4VP for performing the Secure Asset Action on an objective excluding your home objective. This is very simple - you start the action and as long as you control the objective by the end of your turn it completes. This is pretty trivial on all three of the maps. 

You also get 2VP at the end of your turn if you destroy one or more units that are in range of a Central Objective (of which there are two here). On two of the maps (Hammer and Crucible) your opponent is going to pretty much have to move onto one to get their end of turn scoring moving, but be aware that they can do this relatively far away from you, so speedy reach to take them out will be valuable.

Finally, from the second Battle Round onwards, you score 4VP in your Command Phase (or the end of the turn in Round 5) if you hold an objective excluding your home objective, and an additional 4VP if you hold three or more objectives (including your home objective). As with the opponent’s end of turn scoring, you can do the 8VP version while positioned pretty defensively on Hammer/Crucible, but on Dawn of War you’ll find it significantly harder. 

How Your Opponent Scores

Your opponent is also playing for the objectives, but a bit more simply. At the end of each of their turns, they score 4VP for holding three or more Objectives. Because of the maps, this is incredibly trivial for them, and if a Take and Hold-built army is failing to do this, you’ve essentially already won.

Their second Command Phase (or end of turn in Round 5) goal is also pretty easy - hold two objectives (including their home) for 5VP. The second condition is harder though - they need to hold more objectives, which is a pretty big lift for them in anything except Battle Round 5, especially on the Crucible and Hammer Maps. On those, they would have to have pushed into your half of the board, and managed to stay there through your turn, and if they’re pulling that off you’re probably in a lot of trouble resource-wise anyway.

Finally, they get an end-of-game condition, which is that if they hold your home objective, they get 5VP.

Tips for Offense

This mission is a hard one to generalise about, because one of the three maps is extremely not like the others. On Crucible and Hammer, your opponent is forced into a pro-active, beatdown role by simple maths. It’s extremely easy for both players to move onto three objectives, while staying pretty close to their deployment zones. If you both do that and stare at each other, you score 12VP a turn, they score 9VP (both getting 4VP turn 1), so you win. If they’re going second, you have to watch for them finding a route to flip your home objective for a late game push to 45VP, but you will have hit that too, so it’ll come down to Secondaries, and your army is likely better equipped for those.

With that in mind, you have to be prepared for a smart opponent rushing you, so your priority is defence in depth to keep your Expansion objective safe. If you can kill one of their units that’s pushed onto the closer Central Objective each turn to stop you scoring 12s, you still get to 44VP as long as you can lock down your Expansion.

Dawn of War makes things a bit harder, because it’s a great map for rushing people on, and the central objectives are much more evenly placed between the two Deployment Zones. Here, you need to force a flank hard to keep your scoring moving, and one 4/10/10/10/10 becomes your plan A, and you either need to be very fastidious about keeping the opponent to 9s or go on the offensive and try and smash them off the table. The Dawn map is also a bit easier to try and make a play for one turn of stopping their Command Phase scoring completely.

Tips for Defense

The absolutely crucial thing here is that you must stop your opponent from getting any Hold More turns, because if they do that they’re suddenly in the driver’s seat. In addition, stopping them getting 9VP turns is very hard without clean taking them off the table, so defending on Primary can only focus on capping them down to 40VP by ensuring their Hold More never triggers, and whittling away enough of what they’re forced to bring forwards that they have no chance of shooting for your home. 

This means ensuring you have a steady supply of units you can move onto one Expansion and one Central objective. On the Dawn of War map, there are some pretty good staging spots you could use to try and launch one all-out turn to stop them holding any objectives outside their home, so you also have that option if you’re desperate.

You should also just look for an opportunity to spike their army into the dust, and table them. Passive scoring narrowly favours you, but they have more agency to make things swing with a big play, especially at the end of the game. They can’t do that if they don’t have an army any more.

Tips for Listbuilding

A very deep well of 5-model OC2 units will be especially valuable here. Drukhari Wyches or Hearthkyn split by Venoms/Sagitaurs seem especially useful for scoring this one.

Matchup Evaluation

3.5. This matchup is a pretty knife-edge one, because the default scoring is very high for both sides. The steady-state scoring does narrowly favour you, and on two of the maps it feels like you can leverage this to your advantage, and create a battle on your terms, but on the third (Dawn of War) it can be a bit easy for a foe to roll you, and just in general the opponent is slightly freer to just come at you guns blazing to try and force the issue. 

I think it ends up netting out as around a 3.5, because I think this ends up as a knock-down fight a lot of the time, and on two of the three maps you can control the dynamics of engagement.

Vital Link (vs Purge the Foe) 

Purge the Foe vs. Priority Assets

This is a six-Objective mission played on Dawn of War, Hammer and Anvil, or Search and Destroy. The Central objectives are notably all pretty tightly clustered in the centre, unlike two of the Secure Asset ones, and the Search and Destroy map in particular is one of the most vicious thunderdomes in the entire 45-map pack. Having two central objectives is a big help to your plans, but you’ll still need to move where your opponent can see you.

How You Score

The first part of your Primary Mission here is an unusual incrementing mechanic. At the end of your turn, you score 2VP for holding one or more Central Objective, but can get +1VP for each Operation Marker on those objectives. Operation Markers are placed by a one-per-turn action you can perform on a Central Objective and as long as you control it by the end of the turn it completes. This means that every turn you can consistently Action on one of the Central Objectives, you score more VP, so in an ideal world this gets you 3/4/5/6/7, netting you a cool 25 VP. 

You also get 4VP in your Command Phase/Rnd5 EOT for holding one non-home objective, and an additional 4VP if it’s a central objective. 

Finally, if you hold your opponent’s home objective at the end of the game, you get a cool 10VP

How Your Opponent Scores

Your opponent is on a more violent plan. They get 3VP in each of their turns if they destroy one or more of your units, and from the second Battle Round onwards can also score 4VP if they killed more units in their turn than you destroyed in your previous turn. Do note that unlike Kill More missions in 10th, this isn’t counting across the Battle Round, and doesn’t count stuff that gets destroyed by e.g. ongoing combats that falls outside the specific turns where things are counted, so there’s never going to be any risk or variation to the number they need to achieve - and they’ll always know what their target number is. 

Your opponent can also score for objectives. They get 4VP in their Command Phase/Rnd 5 EOT  for holding a non-home Objective, and 6VP for holding more objectives. 

Tips for Offense

Cards on the table: this is going to be rough. This is theoretically a good mission for a very different kind of list than you’re encouraged to build for Priority Assets. A Take and Hold list with a big, super durable tarpit like Necron Warriors or Wraiths? Counting up to 25 and laughing, nabbing an automatic 8VP alongside that in turn 5 (because if you’re getting the action there, it’s a guaranteed hit on the 8VP condition) to make 45VP. Don’t even worry about the opponent’s home. It’s fake and not real.

This is still doable scoring-wise if you have a bunch of MSUs too, but it becomes way higher risk, especially as the expansion objectives here are not especially safe on two of the three maps (Hammer is a bit kinder). You need to very clearly identify which objective you’re trying to hold (it’s probably not a central one) and really lock in on that, and if you fail to do that even once (or judge that you simply can’t), start scheming about how you’re going to stage for a late play to the enemy home.

Your secret weapon is going to be the ability to just keep dropping markers on the same objective, so your goal is to make sure your opponent isn’t move-blocking you off a central objective. 

Tips for Defense

The basic problem here is that you’re damned if you do, damned if you don’t. You can maybe make scoring Kill More tough for the enemy by either pushing with one very durable unit, or at least not throwing too much chaff to the wind. However, if you’re doing that, and the opponent’s list is higher threat than yours, they can probably be bolder about playing for Hold More, and they get a pretty gigantic reward for doing so. I do not have fantastic answers for you here.

Tips for Listbuilding

The challenge for this one is that the ask on you for this and Take and Hold (which we think will probably be the most common early on) are pretty much opposite. Here, if you can have one super durable tarpit unit, it massively smooths things out for you, because you can start pressing the Action button without it being guaranteed to result in Kill More turns for your opponent. We think Purge will be a super common matchup, so you probably should let it shape your list decisions quite a bit - without the right tools, this mission is going to be a nightmare.

Matchup Evaluation

3. This one’s tough to evaluate. It can be as high as 3.5 if you’ve built for it, or as low as a 2 if you haven’t. On paper, you have one of the easiest count-to-45 VP primaries in the pack, but the basic problem is that your opponent’s solution to that is to just kill you. That said, they don’t just have to kill you - they have to block you from moving onto those central objectives. You need to control them to finish your action, but not to start it, and that can give you a lot of leeway to work with. Your passive scoring is better than theirs, but it’s back-loaded. Meanwhile your opponent gets points just for killing your units, and if they can snag Kill More on two or three rounds, they can easily catch up or outpace you.

The maps help you a bit here, giving you two central options to work with, and forcing your opponent to block both if they want to stop you from passively scoring 41 VP on primary. The one to watch out for here is the Search and Destroy map, which is not going to be your friend.

Wings: We had what I shall refer to as a robust internal debate about this one - I think this is much worse for the PA player, especially but not exclusively on the Search and Destroy map. The Purge player has enormous agency to prioritise murdering the PA army as effectively as possible, and there are some major failure cases for the PA player. If they’re not meaningfully on the board in round 5, they lose. If they get blocked out from Actioning once - they probably lose. I have this as a 2 if you’ve built for it, worse if you haven’t.

Sabotage (vs Priority Assets) 

Priority Assets vs. Priority Assets

This is a five-Objective mission played on Sweeping Engagement, Crucible of Battle, or Tipping Point. The central objective on all these maps is a small one, made up of two 6x4 pieces rather than the large 10x10 that most central objective missions have.

How You Score

Unsurprisingly for a mirror match for an Action-heavy Disposition, this one is Action-tastic. At the end of each turn, you score 3VP for each non-home Objective that a unit from your army performs Sabotage on, a standard “hold the objective, completes at end of your turn” Action. For each of these that’s in your opponent’s territory, you get +2VP, and that includes the central objective, since there’s no requirement that the objective be wholly within their territory.

You also get 4VP in your Command Phase/Rnd5 EOT for holding one or more non-home objectives.

How Your Opponent Scores

Exactly the same - it’s a mirror match, after all.

Tips for Offense

This mission is wild in terms of how quickly you can score - it’s completely plausible to hit 45VP in your fourth Command Phase. You make that much easier for yourself if you can find a route to getting a 13VP first turn, and your biggest offensive play here is looking for ways to set that up. Setting Infiltrators or fast Scouts up to push for that is super valuable, because an early 13VP puts you on a pretty solid downhill slope; you only need 12/12/8 afterwards. If you don’t get it, don’t lose heart - you can also realistically hit 45VP in this mission with total 0s for the first two turns (which is extremely unlikely), so if you can’t score early, focus on having the assets alive to score late.

Tips for Defense

Deploy to stop first turn 13VP scoring, for one. This is a mission where using the large engagement ranges units have in 11th Edition to your advantage is very important. Your opponent can only Action if it’s possible to place their models on the objective, so if you stop that, you stem their scoring, even if you lose your unit. You should be especially mindful of this on the central objective, which is pretty small, and if you’re sending some Swooping Hawks onto it that are definitely not going to survive the enemy turn, then consider pushing them onto the opponent’s side of the objective to make it harder for them to walk back on in turn.

Positioning good Overwatch threats overlooking the central objective is also really powerful here - because it’s a small objective with no ruin walls on it, if you stand right next to it you can be protected from distant firepower, but have a clear shot on any opposing model that dares approach. Focusing on killing quantity of enemy units rather than quality will help too - if they run out of spare Action pieces, they’re going to be swiftly out of luck.

Your other option, if you’ve built something super sturdy into your list for the Purge matchup, is to jam that onto the central objective and see what happens. The maps for this make that quite high risk though - you’re going to be eye-wateringly exposed to a full counterpunch from the enemy, and that unit is probably better used lining the top edge of your expansion objective so that it’s simply never in play for the enemy to try and sneak a turn on.

Tips for Listbuilding

This really is one where the more cheap chaff pieces you can assemble into your build the better - running out of those first will be a disaster.

Matchup Evaluation

This is, obviously, a 3.

Extract Relic (vs Disruption) 

Priority Assets vs. Disruption

This is a six-objective mission played with either Sweeping Engagement, Tipping Point, or Search and Destroy deployments. Two of these deployments (Sweeping Engagement and Search and Destroy), put two objectives in each deployment zone, and Tipping Point puts the expansion objectives just outside the zones for easy access. 

At the start of the game, the Disruption player picks five terrain areas outside their Deployment Zone and puts one operation marker on each one. One the layouts provided, that will just be all five of the terrain areas outside their zone (if they can’t, they just put them on however many are outside their deployment zone).

How You Score

Like all Priority Assets missions, this one comes with an Action: Sensor Sweep. Once per turn you can pick a friendly unit within range of one central objective (the way this is phrased they can’t be in range of two), and completes at the end of your turn, if your unit controls that objective. Each time you do this, you remove one operation marker from the battlefield, and you can’t do this action if there’s only one marker on the battlefield. 

Every round, at the end of your turn, you’ll score 4 VP if you did a sensor sweep, 3 VP if you killed an enemy unit that started the turn within range of one or more objectives, and an extra 4 VP if only one of your opponent’s operation markers is on the battlefield AND one or more of your units is within the same terrain area as that operation marker AND no enemy units are within that terrain area.

Starting in the second battle round, in your Command phase (or end of turn in round 5), you’ll score 4 VP for controlling one or more objectives that aren’t your home objective.

At the end of the battle you score 5 VP if only one of your opponent’s operation markers is on the battlefield AND one or more of your units is within the same terrain area as that operation marker AND no enemy units are within that terrain area. This means you can easily score 9 VP if you complete this condition at the end of round 5 if you’re going second.

How Your Opponent Scores

Your opponent can also do the Sensor Sweep action, and each time they do, they remove an operation marker from the battlefield. Similar to Priority Assets, at the end of their turn in any round, they score 4 VP for killing an enemy unit that started the turn on an objective, and 4 VP if there’s only one marker left and they have a unit on the terrain area with that marker and no enemy units are.

Starting in the second battle round, they score 4 VP for holding an objective outside their home, and at the end of the battle they score 5 VP if only one marker is on the battlefield, they have a unit on it, and no enemy units are.

This is a really weird setup, in that you’re both trying to do the same thing but you’re scoring 4 VP for completing the sweep and they aren’t. That said, they score extra VP for kills, and every sweep they do potentially takes 4 VP away from you. If you go first and both players alternate sweeping, then all of the removable sweep markers will be gone by the start of the third battle round. And you can count on their first sweep being the one that removes the marker in your deployment zone. 

Tips for Offense

You want to be the one doing the Sweep Action, scoring VP and choosing which markers get pulled up. Two of these maps make objectives in your opponent’s deployment zone an option; those are going to be your first pulls. If you score Hold One and Kill One in rounds 2-5 you’ll snag 28 VP, and if you can get two Sensor Sweeps in there trading with the opponent you’ll score 36 VP, while if they do the same they’ll only score 32.

Tips for Defense

Your primary goal here is to stop an opponent from performing the Sweep Action, since each time they complete the action they take 4 VP away from you and gives them agency over which markers to remove. In these maps you’re going to have one on your home objective - that’ll be the first one they remove - and one either in your deployment zone or just outside of it. Your goal is to have one of those be where the final operation marker is, and that’ll mean aggressively stopping your opponent from venturing onto central objectives more than once. You want to engagement range block the center so the enemy can’t perform the action, leaving you to dictate the final marker spot. Blocking it turn 1 is a big advantage on that front. 

The player going second can also use some of this to their advantage; if they are ahead on primary or hold the middle they can simply opt to not perform a sensor sweep and keep two markers on the table until it benefits them.

Tips for Listbuilding

Normally Priority Assets is all about getting onto objectives to perform an action but for this mission you also need to be able to move block enemy units to keep them off in the Movement/Shooting phase. That means durable units with 6+ models on larger (32mm+) bases. It’s also another one where a single very tough, high OC unit will serve you well because if you can push onto the middle, do the action, and then the opponent can’t kill that unit it’s catastrophic for their Primary.

Matchup Evaluation

4. This one is a little more complex than it first looks, but we believe it skews toward the Priority Assets player. Scoring 4 VP for each Sensor Sweep action is just really easy, and even if both players trade doing this in rounds 1 and 2, the Priority Assets player can still score 8 VP off it and end up ahead in the primary game against the opponent, forcing the Disruption player to try and aggressively pursue the final objective. This is also a mission where it may be tough to come from behind, since the player in the lead can choose to just not perform a Sensor Sweep and keep markers on the table if removing one would benefit the opponent. There are more ways for the PA player to end up in the driving seat, and that gives them the edge here.

Vanguard Operation (vs Reconnaissance) 

Priority Assets vs. Reconnaissance

This mission gives you a set of three objectives which each use the large, single central objective layout. That tends to make games more deadly, which is going to play into the “kill an enemy” scoring players have access to in this mission. 

How You Score

Much like the other 4 missions described above, you are scoring primary points from actions. This time around you’ll be performing the Vanguard Operation action. The Vanguard Operation action can only be done once a turn and is started in your shooting phase by a unit within one terrain area in your opponent’s territory (your opponent’s half of the battlefield, delineated on the layout). Importantly, the requirement is only within, not wholly within, so the central objective counts. The action completes at the end of your turn as long as there are no enemy units in that terrain area and if successful will net you 4VP. You can do this action every turn. Also every turn that you kill 1+ enemy units, you’ll score 2VP. Starting turn 2 in your command phase (end of turn on turn 5) you’ll score 4VP for controlling 1+ objectives that aren’t your home. At the end of the game you’ll score a huge 10VP if you control your opponents home objective.

How Your Opponent Scores

Your Recon opponent is going to score primary in a semi traditional sense that they score via holding multiple objectives, as well as killing your units kind of hah. So at the end of any of their turns they will score 3VP for holding 1+ Central objectives and/or 2VP for killing 1+ enemy units that started the turn in a terrain area. This is directly counter to your needing to be in terrain to score your Vanguard Operation action points, so keep that in mind. Starting turn 2 in their command phase (end of turn instead of command phase turn 5, as is the standard) they will score 4VP for each objective they control besides their home objective. Additionally at the end of the game they will score 5VP if none of your units are in their territory.

Tips for Offense

Holding extra objectives doesn’t score you extra points, so you are going to have to get your actions complete and get kills. Even if you manage to get a kill and action done every turn you’ll barely cap at 45 points, if you miss a turn of each you’ll cap out at 40 points, which isn’t bad, but if you are going for max score you have to meet the marks. Of course there is always the hail mary of 10 points by holding your opponent’s home objective at the end of the game, but not the most reliable way to score points.

Tips for Defense

Your opponent does score for holding multiple objectives, so don’t let them. They also are getting points for kills from your units in terrain, so make sure whatever units you have doing the Vanguard Operation action in terrain are either safe by being durable or unable to be interacted with. They have to hold central objectives at the end of the turn to reliably keep their primary score up, and it benefits them to kill your units in terrain for points and denying you units to do actions. It looks like this is slightly a razors edge moment, if you consistently stop their hold more, you probably give them a terrain kill every turn, so if they can control a central objective at the end of their turn they are going to nearly max primary. If you are able to have a unit of yours in their territory at the end of the game, you’ll also deny them 5 more points which could very well be the difference in their maxing primary.

Tips for Listbuilding

You are going to need either units that can hide and action or that are straight up durable, you do not want to give away kills if you can help it. You are also still gonna need ways to prevent your opponent from getting hold more and/or units to block out your opponent from getting onto the central objectives.

Matchup Evaluation

3. This match looks like a fairly even playing field, both players have easy access to 40+ VP and can deny the other. You are advantaged if you can end the game in the Recon players territory, especially if you can steal their home, but even with that in mind the Recon player still has a path to 45 points as well. Feels like a mission with outplay opportunities on both sides.

Final Thoughts

Priority Assets averages out at 3.3 (or 3.1 if you believe Wings about the Purge matchup). That makes it an extremely solid choice - it’s above average either way. There are some solid matchups here, and many of the actions are relatively easy to accomplish if you can avoid getting engagement-blocked off terrain. We think this is among the stronger ways to play, and the best of the action-heavy dispositions.
