[40k] Competitive Innovations in 11th: New Era-dication pt.1

by James "One_Wing" Grover | Jun 24 2026
Welcome to Competitive Innovations, our weekly feature for a number of game lines across the tabletop wargame space. Each week our army list and tactics experts canvass the top tournaments for their game of specialty, looking at the week’s top match-ups and how they played out on the tabletop to let you know what’s hot, what’s not, and what might be coming next as the competitive meta evolves year-round.

It's that time again - we've put up the big new sign, we're walking out to fireworks and raucous applause, the crowd are loving it, it's great. There are four 11th Edition events this week, which we've neatly split across the two parts, and as always when a new world dawns, we've brought those to the top because that's what both we and the audience are most interested in digging into. There are also a trio of majors that were played as 10th Edition (because yeah, at major size the logistics of an instant switch doesn't work), which we'll blast through as well. Join us, dear readers, for the dawn of a new age.

A Quick Note: While we upgrade our tech to handle the possibility of multiple Detachments in an army, we'll be making more manual corrections than normal to what gets loaded up into the article shell. Please be patient if we miss a few!

Also - Force Dispositions are now selectable and visible in BCP. If you're wondering where we got them from for lists where it isn't obvious, that's where! Conversely, if we skipped over a Showdown for what looks like no reason, it's probably because the Dispositions weren't set in BCP and weren't visible in lists.

Thoughts

Our first 11th Edition showdown features two of our hot picks for the new edition - fast, furious Mont'ka vs the grindy power and reliability of the Psychic-heavy Thousand Sons. The latter are leaning on one of the spiciest combos available to the Thousand Sons, which is mixing Hexwarp Thrallband and Sekhetar Cohort. Hexwarp powers up your Psychic attacks, and rewards you for holding lots of objectives, Cohort gives Psychic and an improved objective secured Stratagem to your Infiltrators. We love synergy, yes we do. This list plays Take and Hold very well, because it has lots of ways to force opponents to engage on its terms, and a tremendous amount of violence to unleash when they do.

It also doesn't have to give up any easy kills if it doesn't want to, and I think that's its big asset here. The T'au have a solid roster of units to come and fight the Thousand Sons with, and a good amount of mobility, but I think that as long as the Enlightened stay out of trouble early on, the T'au will have to overcommit if they want any kills, and suffer enormous losses in response. They also don't really have the setup you want for trying to fight Scarabs - one Riptide is good, but it's just the one, and you really want access to either more of those or the full missileside squad. I think the T'au's best chance here lies in the sheer disparity of activations between them and the Thousand Sons, and if they're very careful in how they utilise their units, they might be able to put the servants of Tzeentch in a mid-game position where they cannot claim ground fast enough.

I think it's a steep challenge to meet though, especially because of a particularly devious bit of tech in the Thousand Sons list. Tzaangor Shamans have INFANTRY, so are eligible to hide, so I'm assuming the plan with the one here is to join it to a unit, toe just the Shaman onto terrain to claim Hidden and Gone to Ground, then if the opponent gets close to try and pick them off, reactive move away. That provides a pretty solid way to sticky the Tsons expansion objective, at which point with the threat of a big whack of late game points, they can afford to slow roll things and let the T'au come to them, rending them asunder as they do. Given the scoreline, I think that's very likely what went down.

Take a bow our very first winner, covered off in the Showdown. This build is both very powerful in its own right, and a good piece of tech for a (likely) Purge-heavy early metagame, since as covered in the matchup analysis, it has all the tools it needs to really dictate how the game flows against them. The Best of the Rest

There were 4 more players on 4-1 records. They were:

  • 2nd - Kyle Myers - Dark Angels (Company of Hunters/Darkflight Pursuit) - Reconnaissance: An aggressive Ravenwing build packed with bikers and Land Speeders (including several of the attractively-priced new one), and adding the Lion on top for an additional Advance/Charge threat. Speeders with built-in Ignore Cover are very nasty shooting threats, and Samael getting to switch on the detachment rules for his unit (mostly the reactive move Stratagem) also provides a powerful bit of utility.
  • 3rd - Garrett Davies - Emperor's Children (Coterie of the Conceited) - Purge the Foe: A greatly reduced but still nasty monster-skewed Coterie build, revelling in the increased maneuvreability of Fulgrim with new boards and FLY rules, and backing him up with some Winged Daemon Princes, Noise Marines and a Defiler.
  • 4th - Austin Warfel - Tyranids (Subterranean Assault) - Disruption: Sneaky tunnel bugs with a One Eye/double Carnifex unit as a big hammer, and also taking advantage of shockcannon Hive Guard being both better and cheaper in the new world. Also fills out activations for Disruption with some Neurogaunts, and expect to see a lot of people scrabbling around for the cheapest units they can put on the table for some Dispositions.
  • 5th - Logan Smeltz - Salamanders (Forgefather's Seekers/Librarius Conclave) - Purge the Foe: The Salamanders open up new fronts in just how viciously you can incinerate your foes, combining Libarius (mostly for Pyromancy) and Vulkan with big units of Infernus Marines and a Fusillade Sternguard squad. A Land Raider Redeemer also makes an appearence, because honestly with this combo why wouldn't you.

Thoughts

Another Purge/Take and Hold clash for our second showdown, but a lot more evenly balanced here. The Votann definitely do bring grindy power to the table, with the big Hearthguard unit and full grav Thunderkyn squad both having good re-roll access, and able to do horrible things to the Knights. The Knights don't have to take that lying down, however, and the combination of the Ignore Cover Crusader, a Castellan and an Atrapos will carve through anything in the Votann list at speed. The Lancer also provides something a bit sturdier that the Votann can never guarantee a kill on. Finally, the Knights benefit significantly from having a couple of Armigers, which can out-OC a lot of what's in the Votann list unless they commit much more heavily than the really want to. The lines available to a Castellan with Mysterious Guardian on the new tables are pretty brutal, and the Knights definitely want to exploit that.

Like with the other game above, the Votann do still have some agency to control the battle. The Knights have nothing that can benefit from Hidden, so any expansion out onto the table is going to expose them to some amount of retribution. Given that, I think they probably have to swing hard with the Atrapos and Lancer, and hope that it takes the full attention of the Votann to bring one of them down (not unreasonable, with the higher wound count Knights and a Feel no Pain). From there, if the Castellan and Crusader can team up to take down the big Thunderkyn and Hearthguard units, the Knights might overwhelm what's left. If they don't, outlook not so good. This game ended up really close, so I imagine it was a proper knock-down brawl, but the Votann did take it in the end.
